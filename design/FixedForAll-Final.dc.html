01 — Why now

Why this matters now

The law applies now.

Since June 2025, the European Accessibility Act covers online shops, banking, travel booking and many consumer apps.

Scanners only catch part of it.

Automated tools find roughly a third of real problems. Can someone finish your checkout with only a keyboard? Does a screen reader announce errors? That takes a person.

Widgets don’t fix code.

Overlay tools hide problems instead of fixing them. German testing centres (BIK) no longer give their test seal to sites that rely on them.

02 — How it works

Fix it once. Fixed for all.

Most accessibility problems live in a handful of shared components. Fix those, and every page that uses them is fixed.

  1. Step 1

    Check.

    I scan your key pages and test the main user journeys by hand: keyboard only, screen reader, zoom.

  2. Step 2

    Fix.

    I fix problems in your shared components and code, as normal pull requests your team reviews.

  3. Step 3

    Keep it fixed.

    I add simple checks to your release process and give your team a short training, so problems don’t creep back.

08 — FAQ

Questions I get asked

Will this make us compliant?

No one can honestly guarantee that. I test against WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA, the standard the EU law points to, fix what’s broken, and tell you plainly where you stand.

Do you use AI?

I use tools to work faster, but every fix is written into your code, reviewed and tested by a person.

Does the EAA apply to us?

Very small service businesses (under 10 staff and under €2M turnover) are exempt. Most others selling to EU consumers are covered. Happy to check on a call.

What do you need from us?

Access to a test version of your site and, for fixes, your code repository or a way to send pull requests.
